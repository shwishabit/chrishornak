# Evidence for the Authority Check "Experience" checks (Your work shown, Track record)
Researched 2026-10-06 · deep-ish (about 14 sources opened) · by source-researcher

## Answer
- Check 1 (Your work shown) has a strong Google-side source: the Quality Rater Guidelines (Sept 11, 2025) list "Original photos or video footage produced by the website or content creator" under HIGH originality. Google's helpful-content page asks for "first-hand expertise".
- Check 2 (Track record) is thinner. The best source is a rater-guideline example that treats "over 20 years of experience" as grounds for calling someone an expert. No clean consumer-survey number on "years in business" or "number of clients" was found.
- No source below makes a "this ranks higher" claim that we rely on. Ranking-adjacent items are marked "ranking claim, not used".
- All consumer stats are about trust or choosing a business, not rankings. Several are secondary or dated and are flagged.

Conventions: [source] = I opened the page and the text is there. [inference] = my reading. "pdf p." = page number in the PDF viewer (the guideline's own printed page numbers differ by about 1 to 2). Quotes under 30 words. Anything marked PARAPHRASE came back from a fetch summary, not verbatim text, so do not put it in quotation marks on the site.

Notes for the card [inference]:
- The Rater Guidelines are the instructions for Google's human raters; Google says rater scores do not directly change rankings. So the honest wording is "Google's raters are told to look for..." not "Google ranks...".
- "Your own photos" has the best backing (QRG 7.1 "Original photos or video footage produced by the website"). "Real jobs or projects" is backed by the first-hand-experience wording and the Houzz 47% project-scope stat.

### Suggested "Why:" line (check 1)
**Why: Google's rater guidelines rate original photos or footage from the site itself as high originality (Quality Rater Guidelines, 2025).** (19 words)
