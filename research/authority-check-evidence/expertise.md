# Authority Check, Expertise checks: the evidence behind "why this counts"
Researched 2026-10-06 · deep · by source-researcher

## Answer (3-6 lines)
- Google's own documents support all three checks, but only as trust and understanding signals. They never say these checks raise rankings. Google says E-E-A-T "itself isn't a specific ranking factor." [source]
- Real people: the Rater Guidelines tell raters to start at the "About us" page. Consumer data: DreamHost (Nov 2025, n=1,201) 47% pick real team photos as a trust builder. BrightLocal (2014, dated) 64% trust a real owner photo more. NN/G eye-tracking (2010, dated) shows real portraits get attention and stock photos get ignored. [source]
- Credentials: the Rater Guidelines say to judge by "verifiable credentials", not "I'm an expert!" claims. Stanford 2002 (dated) shows listed credentials lift credibility (+1.31) and awards barely do (+0.31). No clean modern study on licenses shown on a small-business site. [source]
- Focus: offer pages: the support is mostly Google structure advice plus an expert opinion survey (Whitespark, ranking claim). No usability study specific to dedicated service pages was found. [source]
- Quotes are exact and under 30 words. Items I did not re-open are marked "from dossier".

Source tags: [source] = I opened it this session. [inference] = my reading. [from dossier] = quote taken from the existing panel dossier, original link kept, page not re-opened. Where the fetch tool returned a summary rather than raw text, that is noted.

## NOT FOUND

- A modern (2019+) controlled study that shows displaying licenses, certifications or memberships on a small-business site raises consumer trust. Looked in: Stanford and NN/G pages, BrightLocal 2023 Trust Report (it covers listings, reviews, photos; staff photos 17% for healthcare and 15% for legal/finance, no credentials), DreamHost 2026, web search for contractor-license surveys. Secondary claims found (HomeAdvisor "82% of homeowners consider licensing important", TrustedSite "84% more likely to trust a site with a trust badge", contractor conversion lifts) were only seen as search-result summaries on blogs and were NOT opened or verified. Do not use them.
- Baymard and Edelman: no study found on credentials, team pages or service pages for small-business sites. The Baymard trust-seal work is about e-commerce checkout, so I left it out. The Edelman figure I saw ("81% say trusting a brand is a deciding factor") is a search snippet, not opened, and it is not about websites.
- A study that directly measures dedicated service pages versus one combined page, on usability, trust or conversions. Looked in: NN/G, Google docs, Whitespark (opinion survey only), Sterling Sky (its research is on Google Business Profile services, not site pages). Not opened: any Sterling Sky page, because the search result gave a summary only.
- Google documentation that says "make one page per service." The SEO Starter Guide sentences found are about general organization and links. Not found in it: any sentence on navigation menus or one-page-per-topic (the fetch tool said so explicitly).
- Rater Guidelines wording on team photos, or on a "team page" specifically. grep for "team page", "photo of" and "staff" found nothing relevant. Only "About us", "who is responsible" and "verifiable credentials" exist.
- Exact page numbers for the awards-as-reputation lines in the Rater Guidelines (section 3.3 area). I saw the matches but did not pin them.
- NN/G sample size for the 2010 eye-tracking photo study. The article does not state one.
- Original, unsummarised text for the DreamHost and BrightLocal pages: the fetch tool returned summaries. The percentages and quoted phrases should be re-checked against the live pages before publishing.
- The Stanford-Makovsky 2002 table for "Other factors" (navigation) is garbled in the PDF text layer, so I did not use its numbers beyond what the report's own prose says.
- Edelman, Trustpilot and Baymard figures on "About" or team pages: nothing specific found.
- Jason Barnard and Lily Ray algorythmic.co quotes, and the Cyrus Shepard Niche Pursuits quote: taken from the panel dossiers, not re-opened.
- Whitespark's report text after character 200,000 was not read (373,084 characters in total). A fuller expert comment on "Dedicated Page for Each Service" may exist there.
- Local-service-page quote from Darren Shaw in his own words: only the score table and the old "myth" sentence exist; no direct statement on service pages.
