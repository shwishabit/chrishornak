Trust 40 points

Reviews on your site15 pts

Why: Reviews from trusted peers are one of the most important things people use to judge a company.

Nielsen Norman Group, "About Us" study, 2019 (70+ users) · Source

Usability studyText checked
Also found
  • BrightLocal 2026 (n = 1,002): 49% trust online reviews as much as people they know. About review sites, not reviews on your own site. Re-check before live. Source
  • Rater Guidelines 2025, section 3.3.2: many detailed, trustworthy, positive reviews count as evidence of a good reputation.

Google tells raters to "be skeptical" of reviews the owner could have written. So the line must not say raters reward testimonials. Real names and real detail matter.

Recently updatedup to 4

Why: Google's raters are told an abandoned website that nobody maintains is a reason for a low quality rating.

Search Quality Rater Guidelines, 2025, p.158 · PDF

Google's own wordsText checked
Also found
  • Stanford Web Credibility, 2002: "People assign more credibility to sites that show they have been recently updated or reviewed." Source (dated)

The same page says "Freshness is generally less of a concern." And no source sets the 2-month and 6-month windows. Those are our own choice, and the "How we score" page should say so.

What the research changes

  1. Cut "Links grow over months, as other sites link to you." No source gives a time.
  2. Say on "How we score" what is our own choice: the 2- and 6-month windows, and that Review spread and Seen elsewhere check links on your site, not the profiles themselves.
  3. Before anything goes live, I re-open the 3 sources marked "Re-check" (BrightLocal, Google 2014, and DreamHost if you use it). Their numbers came through a summary tool, not the page text.

What no one could find

  • A study with a number on "years in business" as a trust factor.
  • A study on testimonials shown on a business's own homepage. All review data is about review sites.
  • A modern study on licenses or certifications shown on a small-business site.
  • A study on press logos, podcasts or associations as trust signals for small businesses.
  • A "Not secure" warning survey we could open.
