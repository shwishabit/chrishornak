Why Google dropped Dusk Route, and how to get it back.

Duplicate data is part of the problem, but it is not the main cause. Three settings on the site hide its best pages from Google. Fix those first, then give Google pages that only Dusk Route could publish.

What is blocking the site

Most serious first
1
Critical

Venue pages are set to noindex.

I checked /sydney/newtown/websters-bar-newtown and /sydney/woollahra/lord-dudley-hotel. Both carry <meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow">, and no venue page is in any sitemap. The homepage lists 2,116 venues. If the noindex is in the venue template, it covers all of them. These pages hold the address, the menu, the deal times and a last-updated date. Google is told to skip them, so it judges the whole site on the template pages.

FixRemove the noindex from venue pages and add them to the sitemap.

2
Critical

robots.txt blocks the CSS and JavaScript.

The line Disallow: /_next/static/chunks/ blocks the files every Next.js page needs to render. Google sees an unstyled, half-built page.

FixDelete that line. Then open URL Inspection in Search Console, click View tested page, and check the screenshot.

3
Critical

Each suburb has up to 4 pages of the same data.

The submitted sitemap lists 358 URLs. 316 of them are suburb pages: 106 suburb pages, plus /statistics and /drink-prices for 105 of them. /queenstown-lakes/arrowtown/statistics has about 115 words, 1 venue and 1 special. /sydney/newtown/drink-prices has 162 words, and most of it is boilerplate.

Two more sitemaps sit outside the sitemap index: sitemap_suburbs_happyhour.xml (245 URLs) and sitemap_regions_happyhour.xml (7 URLs). robots.txt blocks both files. Their /happyhour pages are live and indexable, and they repeat the suburb page. /sydney/alexandria/happyhour has the same meta description as /sydney/alexandria, and about half the same text.

That makes 595 suburb and region pages built from the same data. Hundreds of near-identical pages with little text is the pattern Google's scaled-content filters look for.

Fix301-redirect each /happyhour page to its suburb page, or set its canonical to the suburb page. Then delete the two orphan sitemaps. Merge statistics and drink prices into the suburb page as sections, or noindex them for suburbs with fewer than about 10 venues.

4
Minor

Every sitemap date is the same.

Almost every URL shows the same build time, 2026-10-07T02:46. Google stops trusting lastmod when every page changes at once.

FixSet lastmod to the date the deals on that page changed.

5
Check

Almost no real links, and a pile of spam ones.

Ahrefs shows Domain Rating 1.6 and 286 linking sites. Nearly all of them are one spam network of auto-generated .shop pages ("Buy aggressive crowd links from duskroute.com"). You almost certainly didn't buy them, and Google ignores most links like this. The bigger problem is having almost no real links.

FixOnly disavow these if Search Console shows a manual action for unnatural links.

Fix order

Do these in sequence
  1. Open Search Console, then Security & Manual Actions, then Manual actions. If a penalty shows, make the fixes below and send a reconsideration request. If none shows, the drop is algorithmic: there is no request to send, so make the fixes and wait for a recrawl.
  2. Remove the noindex from venue pages and add them to the sitemap.
  3. Delete the /_next/static/chunks/ line from robots.txt.
  4. Redirect or canonicalize the /happyhour pages to their suburb pages. Merge or noindex the thin statistics and drink-prices pages.
  5. Set real lastmod dates.
  6. Request indexing for your 10 to 20 strongest venue and suburb pages.

What to noindex, and what to keep

Show Google your best pages only

Noindex

  • /happyhour pages. A 301 or a canonical to the suburb page is better than noindex here.
  • /statistics and /drink-prices for suburbs under about 10 venues.
  • Suburb pages with only 1 or 2 venues, until they fill up.
  • /venue-search. It is internal search results.
  • /nearby. It changes with the visitor's location.
  • /map, unless Search Console shows it gets search traffic.

Keep indexed

  • Venue pages, with the noindex removed.
  • Suburbs with real depth. Newtown has 55 venues and 117 specials.
  • Region pages such as /sydney and /blue-mountains.
  • List pages such as /sydney/cheapest-pints. They match what people search for.
  • About, contact, privacy and terms.

Do not also block a noindexed page in robots.txt. Google has to crawl the page to read the tag. Remove every noindexed URL from the sitemap too.

Next: content only you can publish

Once the fixes are in

Google needs pages it can't find anywhere else. You already have the data for them.

  • A monthly price report, such as "What a pint costs in Sydney."
  • A short intro for your top suburbs, written by a person.
  • Descriptions and photos from venues that claim their page.

Next: a few real links

Local sites first
  • Send the price report to Sydney food and drink sites such as Time Out and Broadsheet.
  • Offer venues a "Find our specials on Dusk Route" badge for their own site.
  • Skip paid link packages. They come from the same network already spamming the site.
