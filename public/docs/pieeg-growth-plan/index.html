The first three actions

In order. Each one makes the next work better.
1

Pick the first buyer to win, and say it on the homepage.

The site speaks to researchers, engineers, labs, VR and AI at once. A grad student with $400 can't tell which board to buy. I'd talk to 10 current users from your Discord and testimonials, learn who buys fastest and why they chose you over OpenBCI, then rewrite the homepage and add a short "which board do I need" chooser.

Why first

Every channel after this works better when the message is clear. Ads, posts and outreach all point back to this page.

2

Start collecting buyers before PiEEG XR goes to Kickstarter.

Every sale happens on Elecrow, so today you don't learn who bought. I'd add one signup (the XR launch list, plus a free BCI starter guide built from your existing guides) and a "register your board" step that unlocks the docs and the cloud platform.

Why

A Kickstarter needs people ready on day one. Right now there's nowhere to collect them, and every past buyer is a stranger.

3

Turn the 100+ labs into a repeat channel.

Instructors who teach BCI, neuroscience or biomedical engineering can buy in class sets every semester. I'd build a classroom kit page (Octopus 16 at $250 is the natural entry point), a page listing the papers that use PiEEG, and reach out first to the authors and labs already using it.

Why

One instructor can become a standing order. Your current users are the proof, so the first conversations start warm.
