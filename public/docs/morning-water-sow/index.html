Your new product page, live and tested before the stock lands.

October 1 is not a date someone picked. It is the day your page starts taking money again. So it has to be finished before the inventory arrives, not alongside it.

What is on the page right now

Checked today

Your banner says you are sold out. Your hero says it ships in 24 hours. Both sit on the first screen.

The kit badge reads $139 Value. Your own notes put it at $85. It reaches $139 by counting the $89.99 supply as a free gift.

The page claims 5.0 stars. Your review export averages 4.78. Your new mock already says 4.8, so the mock is right and the live page is not.

Your hero image has $2.31 per day baked into the picture. The new plan cards say $2.10 per serving.

Two of your three testimonial cards are still placeholder names and placeholder photos.

None of this is a design problem. Your design is settled. It is a consistency problem, and that is what two weeks of focused work fixes.

What I would do about it

Build the buy box from your 4d mock, desktop and mobile. Reorder the page and cut the ticker. Fix the three rendering faults. Make every number agree. Then turn sold out and in stock into a switch you flip, so the page can never argue with itself again. Test it on real phones, walk checkout end to end, and hand it over written down so your team can run it without me.

Why two weeks is real

I have already been through your theme. It is custom sectioned, not page built, which is the whole reason this fits.

Your buy box lives in product-hero, and that section already carries a waitlist state and a try-once label in its settings. The page assembles from kit-products, testimonials-carousel, comparison-table and the rest.

I am working with sections you already own. I am not rebuilding a template.

What I would leave alone

Your cost comparison table is the best built thing on the page. Comparing against seven supplements bought separately is stronger and more honest than comparing against a rival stick.

The Science of Synergy is the second best. Both stay as they are, apart from the number sweep.

Anyone can hand you a list of problems. Knowing what not to touch is the harder half.
