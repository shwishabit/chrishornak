Morning Water · product page · 17 September 2026

The new product page, live and tested before the stock lands.

October 1 is not a deadline someone picked. It is the day the page starts taking money again. So the work has to finish before the inventory arrives, not alongside it — every day the page is late is a day of restock demand landing on the old one. This document is what I will build, in what order, what I need, and what it costs.

Deliverables

Included in both options
01

Buy box rebuilt to your 4d mock

Eyebrow, title, rating, paragraph, the five bullets, both plan cards, the Ritual Kit panel, Add to Cart, trust row, the try-once line and the study microcopy. Desktop and 390px, built against your existing Skio selling plans.

02

Gallery and thumbnail strip

Reordered to the mock, kit photo leading, thumbnails behaving on touch.

03

The three rendering faults corrected

Clipped portraits, the cut-off header photo, and text overlapping the image — fixed across breakpoints, not just on one screen size.

04

A stock switch, not a stock edit

Sold-out and in-stock as two states you toggle from the theme editor. Today the banner and the hero contradict each other; after this, they cannot.

05

Numbers reconciled in the buy box and kit

Kit value, per-serving price, plan pricing and badges agreeing with each other. Your notes put the kit at $85; the live badge still reads $139 and counts the supply itself as a gift.

06

Tested on real devices, in both states

iOS and Android, sold-out and in-stock, subscription flow walked through to checkout. Page speed checked against the current page so the rebuild does not cost you load time.

07

Written handover

Short and plain: what changed, which settings control what, and how to flip the stock switch — so your team can edit the page without me.

08

Two days of aftercare

Anything that surfaces in the 48 hours after launch is mine to fix, at no charge.

Not included

Said plainly, so nobody assumes

New photography or the replacement hero graphic · copywriting from scratch · the 5-stick trial SKU and the nurture flow from the August audit · other templates (cart, collection, account) · changes to your Skio plan structure or pricing · app subscriptions and fulfilment. I will spec anything here that the build needs, and place whatever you supply.

Done means

How you check my work, not how I describe it

The page tells one story in either state. Flip the stock switch and every line on the first screen agrees with every other one. You can test this yourself in under a minute.

The buy box matches the mock at both sizes, and the subscription flow completes through to checkout on a real phone — not a simulator.

No number contradicts another. Kit value, per-serving price, plan pricing and badges all say the same thing wherever they appear.

Your team can run it without me. Stock state, plan copy and kit contents are all editable from the theme editor after handover.

On the Shopify side

I have been through your theme. It is custom-sectioned rather than page-built, which is the reason two weeks is realistic — the buy box lives in product-hero, which already carries a waitlist state and a try-once label in its settings, and the page assembles from kit-products, testimonials-carousel, signs-grid, ingredients-grid-v2, comparison-table and the rest. I am working with sections and settings you already own, not rebuilding a template from scratch.

Most recent comparable engagement: gammalighttherapy.com — Shopify Liquid theme and section work alongside search and content, as an independent contractor rather than through an agency. Same shape of problem: a live store, an owner who needed the work taken off their hands, and changes that had to hold up in production.

How we work together

Where. Slack or Google Chat, whichever you already live in. Email for anything that needs a record. Scheduled calls when a decision needs a conversation rather than a thread.

How often. A short written update at the end of each phase, plus a preview link from day two so you can watch it grow rather than wait for a reveal.

What I need on day one. Shopify collaborator access with theme permissions, and whoever owns Skio and Junip reachable on a thread. This is the item that can stall everything behind it.

Decisions. Seven content items are still open on your side — they are listed in the project brief. None block the start; all of them block the finish.

What could go wrong, and what I do about it

Access or decisions arrive late. This is the likeliest one. The launch moves day for day, and I tell you the day it is at risk rather than the day it slips.

The hero image cannot be re-exported in time. Fallback is a clean crop with the claims set in live text instead. We decide that on the 22nd, not the 30th.

Skio or Junip behaves differently in the new markup. This is exactly why phase one sits on a development theme with a preview link from day two — surprises surface in week one, not launch week.

Something turns out bigger than it looked. I say so before I start it, with what it would take. I do not quietly absorb it and miss the date, and I do not invoice you for a surprise.

The short version

The design is settled and the theme is already built for this. What is left is execution, consistency and a date. Say go by Monday and the page is live, tested and handed over before your stock arrives.

To start: reply with the option you want and I will send a one-page agreement and the first invoice the same day.
