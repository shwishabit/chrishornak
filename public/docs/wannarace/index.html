Wannarace, refreshed

I worked from what you already have: your name, your four kit colors, your checkers and your WR. Four ways to refresh it. Each one has its own logo, colors and fonts, shown on a jersey, an Instagram post and race-day gear.

One thing I noticed: the WANNARACE wordmark on your site and the WR badge on the kit use two different letter styles. One is squared and sharp, the other is rounded. Each direction below brings them into one family, so the logo, the badge and the jersey read as one brand.

Concept 2 · Your look, pushed further

Hot Laps

Central Park before sunrise. Your colors with black in the lead, built around one idea: the W is a zigzag.

Logo suite

Why it works
The zigzag stops being a stripe and becomes the logo itself. One shape works as the badge, the jersey graphic and the social pattern, so the system is easy to extend to new jerseys and merch.

A black jersey runs hot in summer. A white version of the same system would sit next to it in the line.

Concept 3 · Built from your site font

Tire Tracks

Every ride leaves a mark. The headline font from your website becomes the logo, and the tire track becomes the pattern.

Logo suite

Speaks to
The riders you coach who log the miles, beginners to racers.
Why it works
Your website already sets its headlines in Oswald. Making it the logo too means your site, your kit and your posts share one face. The tire track works as a full print, a stripe or a small tag.
Watch out
Tire tracks show up on other bike brands, so the tread should be drawn from a tire your team actually races on.
