Wannarace, refreshed

I worked from what you already have: your name, your four kit colors, your checkers and your WR. Four ways to refresh it. Each one has its own logo, colors and fonts, shown on a jersey, an Instagram post and race-day gear.

One thing I noticed: the WANNARACE wordmark on your site and the WR badge on the kit use two different letter styles. One is squared and sharp, the other is hand-lettered and rounded. Each direction below settles that in its own way, so the logo, the badge and the jersey read as one brand.

Concept 2 · Built from your site font

Tire Tracks

Every ride leaves a mark. A font your website already uses becomes the logo, the tire track becomes the pattern, and black takes the lead.

Why it works
Your website already uses Oswald Bold. Making it the logo too means your site, your kit and your posts share one face. The tire track works as a side-panel print, a band on the bottle and the socks, and the ring of the badge.

Concept 3 · Your own WR, drawn clean

Kit Script

The hand-lettered WR on your jersey is the one mark no other team has. Here it is redrawn as one clean shape and stays the lead, large on the chest and the back.

Logo suite

Speaks to
Your team, and every rider who already knows the WR from the road.
Why it works
This is the smallest change of the four. I redrew your WR stroke by stroke from your team photos, so it is one sharp shape on a straight baseline that prints clean from a sleeve to a full back. The name sits next to it in a squared, slanted sans, like the small WANNARACE tag already on your jersey. The stripes are cut at the same angle as the strokes of the W.
Watch out
The WR sits across the zip, as it does on your kit today, so the two front panels must line up at the loop. The maker handles that with alignment notches at the zip. The script is too fine to knit, so the socks carry the stripes. If you have the original WR file, send it and I will match it exactly.
