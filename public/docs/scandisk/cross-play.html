A · BAD SECTOR

Turn-based tactics. Two programs, no attack button. You only shove.

play →manual →

Where all three agreed

  • The forecast cannot lie, and that is the best code in the jam. forecast() runs the real resolve() and draws what came back. All three called it out unprompted. C: "best single engineering decision." D: "the strongest single line of code in the jam."
  • The random crack tile is the one unowned die. Every round a hole opens on a uniformly random open tile. All three flagged it: this is the only game promising perfect information, and once a round something happens that no amount of reading can anticipate. Three different fixes offered: let the virus aim it (C), crack the tile a red died on (D), or replace it outright (B).
  • Two win conditions race, and the quiet one always loses. The screen promises "keep every file alive until the BACKUP bar fills" — but the bar is set to 8 rounds and the median board ends at 4. You actually win by exterminating. B, C and D each arrived at this separately. D put it hardest: nothing counts up, and waiting out the timer prints the same CLEAN as wiping the board.
  • The DANGER line is the wrong shape. All three hit the same HUD row. It gives you the count and withholds the name — and the game already knows the name.
  • Contested: C wants UNDO replaced with instant RESTART ("undo is restart with the pain smeared out"). B and D both named UNDO as a thing they liked.
TEAM B · REAL TIME — Jonasson, the Pfeiffers, Adams, Fumoto

An 8x6 grid where two programs get three steps each, your only weapon is shoving a red into a wall, and the board shows you the whole next round before you commit.

Like

  • The forecast cannot lie, by construction. rules.ts:177 — forecast() runs the real resolve() and returns its log. A system showing its own working is exactly what our school wants.
  • No attack button. Arrows are the whole verb set; walk and shove are the same press, decided by what is next to you. One input, four consequences.
  • The teaching board has no dice in it. pickCrack returns -1 while teaching, and the map is hand-built. teach.png shows the money move as move one, guaranteed, every run.
  • Z UNDO is on the pad, labelled, at phone width. A turn game that lets you step back is a turn game that lets you think instead of guess.
  • The goal is named before move one — demo-goal.png holds it 2.6s. Most tutorials teach the verbs and never the ask.

Don't like

  • The stated goal is not the real goal. tune2a.ts:4 sets maxRound: 8, but the sim's median board ends at round 4. The screen promises survival; the maths pays extermination.
  • Worm and spike read as one red mass from arm's length. Two units with completely different behaviour, one silhouette.
  • The loudest thing on the board is the cheapest rule. The cracked tile is uniform random, yet it out-shouts the spike.
  • DANGER: 1 OF YOURS DIES ON START gives the count and withholds the name. The one fact the forecast refuses to hand over is the one you would act on.
  • The manual's winning shot reads SECTOR 00. scenes.ts:31 never sets g.sector, so the victory screenshot shows a sector that cannot exist in play.

Would change

  • Pick one win condition. Either stop the board ending on the last crash so the bar means something, or cut maxRound to the real median. Right now two win conditions race and the quiet one always loses.
  • Give spike and worm different shapes, not different red glyphs. Shape carries across a room; hue does not.
  • Aim the crack instead of rolling it. Open it under the tile the virus most wants, and it joins the rest of the board as something you can read.

Would add (each replaces something)

  • Name the file in the danger line: DANGER: MOM.WAV DIES ON START. Replaces the count line. Same row, same pixels, a decision instead of a jump-scare.
  • A BACKUP +1 tile a program can stand on to fill the bar faster. Replaces the random crack. The bar becomes something you play toward rather than something that expires behind you.
Their question for you after board 1When you won — did the backup bar fill, or did you kill the last red? And which one did you think you were playing for?

Their stated bias: over-values systems that keep running while you look away, and boards readable from across the room. Under-values pleasure in the exact instant of a press.
