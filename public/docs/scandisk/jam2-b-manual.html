The six rules

The game itself no longer shows these. On the way in you get three ideas — the rot never stops, the files go to the port, a man carries or holds and cannot do both — and then the demo plays a board. Six numbered rules in front of a stranger is a wall, and if the demo works you do not need them. They live here instead, in full. (team call)

  1. Real time. Red takes a cell every few seconds and never stops. The more files it eats, the faster it spreads.
  2. One order. Move the cursor with the D-pad. X sends your picked worker there. Z picks another worker.
  3. Carry. A worker who steps on a file copies it (a few seconds, standing still), then carries it to the PORT by himself. That file is SAVED.
  4. Wall. Red cannot enter a worker's cell. It bites him instead and loses that tick. Three bites and he is gone for good.
  5. Loss. Red on a file eats it. A carried file dies with its worker.
  6. The end. Files surface one at a time. When the last of the eight is saved or eaten, the board ends. Save 4 to keep the sector.

The tutorial plays itself

Round 2 cut the hands-on gym. In its place the game plays a board in front of you and says why. It takes about 25 seconds and it teaches three things in order: a carry, a block, a save.

  1. Four opening lines name the pieces: the port, red's next cell, your two men.
  2. "SEND THE LOW MAN AT MOM.WAV." — and he goes. Every line is said, then the rules do exactly that.
  3. "RED WANTS THAT HOLLOW BOX. GET THERE FIRST." The second man stands on it. Red bites him and loses its whole turn. That is the block, named out loud.
  4. "PULL HIM OUT. THREE BITES AND HE IS GONE." Then the first man lands MOM.WAV at the port.
  5. Any button takes the board over right where it stands. START skips the lesson for good.
  6. At the end: the same board, fresh, with a card. YOUR TURN. Save 2 of 4.

The clock, and what went wrong first. SALVAGE is the only real-time game in the jam. The first build of this demo froze the world on every narrated line so the words could not fall behind. Red grows constantly, so a line fired nearly every frame, each one bought another two seconds of freeze, and the world got one frame of motion per freeze — the men never moved, nothing was ever saved, and the demo could not reach its own ending. Thresh gated it: do not ship. The rebuild does the opposite. The world never stops; it runs at 0.55× throughout. The demo is a scripted list of beats — a line, the order it describes, and the thing that must become true before the next line — so the lesson is certain instead of hoped for. Every cap is in real seconds, not world seconds. (team call)

The teaching board

Two men, four files, and a 2×3 rot mass parked one cell short of TAXES.XLS. Because red prefers a fat front, that shape makes its next cell the same cell every tick — which is what lets the demo promise a block instead of hoping for one. Save 2 of 4. It is a real small game, not a sign. (team call)

The one idea

A worker is either a carrier or a wall, never both. You only have four of them, so every rescue opens a hole and every hole you plug is a file you are not saving. That single trade-off is the whole game.

Round 2 found that the wall half was a lie. The sim counted the bot's block orders for the first time: 0 in 30 boards. A carry always outscored a block, so nobody ever held a line — the trade-off was a sentence in this manual, not a decision. The rules were fine; the bot was pricing the verb wrong. A bite cancels red's whole push for that tick, so a block is now priced by the cells it cancels, and a man can get set on the wider rot front instead of only on the one cell red takes next. Blocks per board went 0.00 → 2.27, files saved went 3.17 → 3.30, and a real choice went 54% → 70%. On screen, a man who is about to be bitten now wears two pale-blue brackets: he is a wall this tick.
