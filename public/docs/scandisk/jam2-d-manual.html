SCANDISK · jam 2 · team D (RUN + HAND) · 2026-09-19

The drive is dying. You copy files off it, and one long chain saves a whole sector at once.

The story

Press START on the title and this comes up before the rules. Five lines, one at a time, all of them on screen inside two seconds. A press while they are still arriving drops the rest in at once — it takes a second press to move on, so mashing START never skips the screen.

The six rules

  1. Hit the target before the drive fills. The blue bar on top is what you saved. The red bar under it is the drive filling up.
  2. Hand of 3. You spend 2 every turn. The 3rd stays for next turn. There is no "end turn" button — the turn ends when your 2 cards are gone.
  3. X saves a card. Bytes = size × chain. Save the same file type again and the chain goes up. Save a different type and it drops back to x1. The green number on each card is what it pays right now.
  4. The virus shows its next 2 cards. The big one fires at the end of this turn. It floods the drive by its number, and if its type matches your chain, it breaks the chain.
  5. Z blocks with a card. The card's size cuts that flood, size for size. Cut it all and the chain is safe too.
  6. Target met: take 1 of 3 new cards into your deck. 4 sectors and the drive is saved. Drive full and the run is over.
The whole game is one question, every turn: cash the chain in now, or spend a card to protect it for one more turn?

The first ten seconds: the game plays itself first

Title → Enter → the story → Enter → the six rules → Enter → SAFE MODE, the teaching board. You do not play it yet. The game plays it for you.

The bot that runs our sim takes the board and plays it slowly, on screen, saying one plain thing per move: which card, and why. Then it stops on the card it did not play, puts that card under its own border and a KEPT tag, and tells you why it is holding it — because that is the move a card game never shows you. When the demo is done, the same board with the same hand is handed back to you. (team call, round 2.)

The teaching board's opening hand and its refills are scripted, so this is the same run for every player. Every number in the shots below is the number you will see.

Press any button during the demo to take the board over mid-move — the screen says YOUR TURN. Press Enter to skip the whole demo and get the board fresh.

The big moment, and the end

A chain carries across turns. Protect it with blocks for two or three turns, keep feeding it one type, and then a size-3 card lands for twenty-odd bytes and clears the sector in one press.

A run always ends because the virus's numbers climb. Two size-3 cards can block 6; by round ten or so the flood is bigger than that, and nothing holds it.

Team calls (Chris to rule on)

  • Spend exactly 2 cards a turn. This replaced an "end turn" button and forced a real keep-one decision every turn.
  • The deck is a bag, drawn with replacement. No discard pile, no reshuffle — one fewer rule. Cards you take between sectors change the odds, not a shuffle order.
  • A block only stops flood up to the card's size. Big cards are both your big scores and your big shields.
  • Blocking does not touch the chain — only a save moves it.
  • The virus card is fully derived from the sector and the round, so the two cards on screen are never a lie.
  • SAFE MODE never ends a run. Fail it and it restarts.
  • Round 2: the hands-on gym was deleted and replaced by the self-playing demo. You watch the bot play the teaching board, then get the same board.
  • Round 2: SAFE MODE's virus now HAS a file type, on a fixed four-card script. It used to be typeless so a beginner could not lose a chain — but a typeless virus also makes the shield idea invisible, and the shield is the one thing nobody works out alone. A real threat is what the demo points at. This replaced the old rule, it did not stack on it.
  • Round 2: the teaching board's draws are scripted, not random. Pinning only the opening hand left the headline beat to the run seed. Now the refills are pinned too, so the demo is the same run — and the same numbers — every time.
  • Round 2: one bot brain. The bot that teaches you on screen is the same file the sim measures (src/jam2d/bot.ts). If they drifted apart, the demo would be a lie.

What we cut on the way: an "eats your hand" virus card (replaced by "breaks your chain"), a discard-and-reshuffle deck, special card powers, an END TURN button, a fourth file type, the hands-on gym, and the typeless SAFE MODE virus.

Measured and thrown away (round 2): we raised every sector's opening flood to force the save-or-block call earlier. The win rate fell from 9/30 to 2/30 and the strict real-choice rate barely moved (42% → 45%). It made the game harder, not deeper, so we reverted it. The round-1 numbers stand.
